The Bisection Method

A comprehensive mathematical and engineering exploration of Bolzano's classical bracket bisection algorithm applied to fluid mechanics and transient water tank dynamics.

1. Mathematical Foundation: Bolzano's Theorem

Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

The Bisection Method is a closed bracketing numerical technique rooted in Bolzano's Intermediate Value Theorem. Let a real-valued function \( f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R} \) be continuous on the closed interval \([a, b]\).

Intermediate Value Theorem Condition
\[ \text{If } f \in C[a, b] \quad \text{and} \quad f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0, \quad \exists \, t^* \in (a, b) \; \text{such that} \; f(t^*) = 0 \]

In physical terms, since the volume of water in a tank \( V(t) \) varies continuously over time \( t \), if at time \( t = a \) the water level is below the target level (\( f(a) < 0 \)), and at time \( t = b \) the water level exceeds the target level (\( f(b) > 0 \)), then by physical continuity there must exist at least one instant \( t^* \in (a, b) \) where the water precisely reaches the target level.

2. Tank Filling Mathematical Formulation

From Differential Continuity to Root Formulation

Consider a water tank of total capacity \( V_{\text{max}} \) with initial fluid volume \( V_0 \). Water is pumped into the tank at a constant or head-dependent volumetric inflow rate \( Q_{\text{in}} \), while an optional bottom orifice drains fluid at rate \( Q_{\text{out}} \).

The instantaneous rate of change of water volume in the reservoir is governed by the mass balance differential equation:

Continuity Mass Balance
\[ \frac{dV}{dt} = Q_{\text{in}}(t, V) - Q_{\text{out}}(t, V) \]

Under uniform flow conditions with net volumetric rate \( Q_{\text{net}} = Q_{\text{in}} - Q_{\text{out}} \), the total water volume at time \( t \) is given by:

Volume Function V(t) & Objective Root Function f(t)
\[ V(t) = V_0 + (Q_{\text{in}} - Q_{\text{out}}) \cdot t \] \[ f(t) = V(t) - V_{\text{target}} = V_0 + (Q_{\text{in}} - Q_{\text{out}}) \cdot t - V_{\text{target}} = 0 \]

Under the nonlinear Hydrodynamic Head Loss Model, pump delivery decreases exponentially as hydrostatic backpressure accumulates:

Nonlinear Hydrodynamic Model
\[ f(t) = V_{\text{max}} - (V_{\text{max}} - V_0) \, e^{-\frac{Q_{\text{net}}}{V_{\text{max}}} t} - V_{\text{target}} = 0 \]

3. The Bisection Algorithm Mechanics

Algorithmic Pseudo-code and Decision Logic
Algorithm: Bisection Numerical Root Finder
Input: Continuous function \( f(t) \), initial brackets \( [a_0, b_0] \), tolerance \( \epsilon \), max iterations \( N \)
Output: Estimated root \( c \approx t^* \)
1. Verify \( f(a_0) \cdot f(b_0) \le 0 \). If false, abort bracket invalid.
2. For \( k = 1, 2, \dots, N \) do:
    a. Compute midpoint: \( c_k = \frac{a_{k-1} + b_{k-1}}{2} \)
    b. Compute error: \( \epsilon_k = \frac{b_{k-1} - a_{k-1}}{2} \)
    c. Evaluate \( f(c_k) \)
    d. If \( \epsilon_k < \epsilon \) or \( |f(c_k)| < \epsilon \):
        Return \( c_k \) (Root found with required accuracy)
    e. If \( f(a_{k-1}) \cdot f(c_k) < 0 \):
        \( a_k = a_{k-1} \), \( b_k = c_k \) (Root lies in left half)
      Else:
        \( a_k = c_k \), \( b_k = b_{k-1} \) (Root lies in right half)
3. Return \( c_N \) with warning (Max iterations exceeded).

4. Convergence Analysis & Theoretical Error Bound

Rate of Convergence and A-priori Step Estimation

At each iteration step \( k \), the length of the bracket interval is strictly halved:

Interval Length Decay
\[ \Delta_k = b_k - a_k = \frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^k} \]

Because the true root \( t^* \) is guaranteed to lie within \([a_k, b_k]\), the absolute distance between the midpoint approximation \( c_k \) and the true root \( t^* \) satisfies:

Absolute Error Bound
\[ |c_k - t^*| \le \frac{b_k - a_k}{2} = \frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^{k+1}} \]

To guarantee an accuracy tolerance of \( \epsilon \), we require:

A-Priori Minimum Iteration Formula
\[ \frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^n} \le \epsilon \implies 2^n \ge \frac{b_0 - a_0}{\epsilon} \implies n \ge \left\lceil \frac{\ln(b_0 - a_0) - \ln(\epsilon)}{\ln 2} \right\rceil \]

Key Computational Insights:

  • Order of Convergence: Linear convergence with asymptotic rate \( C = \frac{1}{2} \). Each step yields approximately 1 bit of binary precision (or 1 decimal place every \( \approx 3.32 \) iterations).
  • Unconditional Reliability: Unlike Newton-Raphson, Bisection never diverges or oscillates provided \( f(t) \) is continuous and bracketed.
  • Zero Derivative Requirement: Does not require analytical or numerical computation of \( f'(t) \).
